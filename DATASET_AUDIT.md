# Dataset and notebook audit

Audit date: 2026-11-22. The original notebook remains unchanged. The workspace initially contained only the notebook; no source data were available locally. Record counts below are publisher-reported unless stated otherwise, not locally verified corpus counts.

## Notebook findings

The notebook loads `cnamuangtoun/resume-job-description-fit` train and takes at most 500 pairs. It discards short records, deduplicates on the first 200 lowercased characters of the resume, and cuts resumes to 1024 characters and jobs to 512. This can discard distinct resumes and genuine relationships. No group or near-duplicate leakage audit is performed. Training sources are capped to 800 and randomly split by pair rather than grouped; that split's validation pairs are not the actual retrieval evaluation. Cross-dataset overlap is unknown.

The loader expects `resume_text` and `job_description_text`, without inspecting original labels. Its two training datasets are queried under `input`/`output` and `resume`/`job_description`/`fit` respectively. The Philseok viewer actually shows `resume_text`, `job_description_text`, and `label`, so that loader silently skips records. For netsol, the documented `aggregated_scores` is nested under `output.scores`, whereas the notebook looks directly under `output`, producing incorrect defaults.

The semantic evaluation assumes every source row is relevant and adds labels using cosine similarity of resume embeddings, then evaluates a system using those same embeddings. This is circular and does not establish independent relevance. The separate self-retrieval benchmark queries resume text against itself and is an infrastructure sanity check only. Metrics assume binary exhaustive labels and treat unknown pairs as irrelevant; duplicate retrieved IDs are not explicitly guarded against. LambdaRank assigns one sample per query group, precluding pairwise learning; training uses a TF-overlap proxy while inference uses actual corpus BM25, so the feature distributions differ. Prefix-limited cross-encoder caching can collide. There is no valid basis for interpreting scores as hiring probabilities. The UI contains fixed performance claims and calls the output a match percentage.

Saved outputs show a previous Colab GPU execution, not a reproducible local experiment. Notebook printed scores and hardcoded UI metrics are not independent evidence of semantic performance. Exact output and record counts should be rechecked during a complete corpus audit; no numbers are claimed as new results.

## Dataset provenance

* [cnamuangtoun/resume-job-description-fit](https://huggingface.co/datasets/cnamuangtoun/resume-job-description-fit): referenced primary corpus. Its suitability, provenance, license, actual schema, annotation generation and rights to send text externally must be verified before use. No automatic selection has been made.
* [netsol/resume-score-details](https://huggingface.co/datasets/netsol/resume-score-details): documentation describes GPT-4o generated job descriptions and GPT-4o judgments; excluded under the user requirements. Publisher reports 1,031 files (201 mismatched, 648 matched, 142 invalid, 40 missing additional info), not independently verified.
* [Philseok/resume_fit_data](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Philseok/resume_fit_data): viewer reports 334 training rows and columns `resume_text`, `job_description_text`, `label`. Authenticity, license, collection and judgment provenance remain unverified; do not treat labels as independent.
* Potential replacement: [vacancy-resume-matching-dataset](https://github.com/NataliaVanetik/vacancy-resume-matching-dataset) describes human ground truth, includes annotation instructions, and displays GPL-3.0. Authenticity of actual documents, detailed annotation provenance, accessible schemas, counts, and rights still require verification. It is a candidate, not an approved substitution.

No primary dataset has been selected because the mandatory authenticity, licensing and annotation checks are incomplete. Human annotation and semantic benchmarks remain pending. Do not use data externally until privacy and license requirements have been checked.

The primary dataset viewer currently reports about 6.24k training rows with `resume_text`, `job_description_text`, `label` and 642 resume / 280 job text values. These are viewer-reported figures, not locally audited exact counts. The replacement README states 65 anonymized CVs and five vacancies, with rankings from two human annotators; the exact annotation files and underlying collection/rights still require inspection.

## 2026-11-22 follow-up: selected local research corpus
The [authors' paper](https://www.mdpi.com/2078-2489/14/8/468) describes real resumes downloaded from an open Telegram employment group, filtered for English developer resumes, deduplicated and anonymized. Vacancies came from data.world. The [repository](https://github.com/NataliaVanetik/vacancy-resume-matching-dataset) has a GPL-3.0 LICENSE; underlying job-post and resume rights, consent and permission to transmit to a third-party API are not established. Use this corpus locally for research; do not send its resumes to Groq without an independent privacy and rights review. Selection is for local experiments, not a claim that all third-party reuse is permitted.

Local checkout contains 65 DOCX files and a CSV of five vacancies with columns id, job_description, job_title, uid. The annotation file describes rankings of five vacancies for each of the first 30 CVs by two independent annotators using senior-HR guidance. This is resume-to-job ranking, not direct job-to-resume labels. Some arrays contain repeated entries, and the README and annotation filename disagree. Do not silently reinterpret or aggregate them; investigate annotation mapping and ties before relevance evaluation. The sample is small, developer-focused and nonrepresentative, and pre-filtering introduces selection bias. No independent relevance results are claimed.
- Local exact-text audit of converted corpus: 70 documents, 357 chunks, zero blank documents and zero exact duplicate-text groups. Near-duplicate and split leakage audits remain pending.

## 2026-11-22 larger primary corpus: Djinni
Selected the original researchers' [English candidates](https://huggingface.co/datasets/lang-uk/recruitment-dataset-candidate-profiles-english) and [English jobs](https://huggingface.co/datasets/lang-uk/recruitment-dataset-job-descriptions-english), language partitions of the single Djinni corpus. Their [paper](https://aclanthology.org/2024.unlp-1.2/) describes real 2020–2023 Djinni IT platform postings and states MIT licensing. Hugging Face metadata also marks both MIT. Downloaded originals at revisions candidates `86255174c6c378a2b52cd7e81d79002c64d899a6` and jobs `b56a6054c10f1266a141e37c8df66c79ff2863af`. Local Parquet metadata reports 210,250 candidates and 141,897 jobs, with candidate fields `id`, `CV`, `Position`, `Moreinfo`, `Looking For`, `Highlights` and job fields `id`, `Long Description`, `Position`, `Company Name`, among others. The importer preserves the source CV or job description and source IDs; it does not claim these fields encompass all structured source metadata.

The corpus has no verified pairwise relevance judgments for this benchmark. Do not infer labels from shared categories, embedding similarity or original ordering. The paper reports residual protected attributes, including apparent names, after anonymization. MIT licensing does not establish blanket consent or rights to transmit identifiable information to an external API: keep candidates local. The first-N benchmarking subset is nonrandom and cannot support population or relevance claims. Retain the smaller Vanetik/Kogan dataset separately as a limited research benchmark; do not merge labels across corpora.

## Seeded-sample duplicate and leakage audit

The current benchmark sample is `data/processed/djinni_random_1729.jsonl`: uniformly sampled without replacement from original Parquet row positions with seed 1729 (10,000 profiles, 200 jobs). The adjacent manifest records source file SHA-256 hashes, row-position hashes, counts, Python version and the sampling algorithm. Recomputing the selected positions matched both partitions. Prior source-ordered artifacts remain separate.

Among these 10,200 sampled documents, normalized exact comparison found zero within-kind duplicate groups and zero cross-kind exact-text groups. A high-similarity screen compared 3,194 candidate pairs generated using four 16-bit bands of 64-bit SimHash, retained Hamming distance at most 3, and checked 3-token shingle Jaccard >= 0.8; it found zero near-duplicate pairs. This is a candidate-filtered screen and can miss near duplicates, including high-Jaccard pairs with larger SimHash distance; it is not an exhaustive or full-dataset audit. The sample shares 518 upstream IDs with the old source-ordered sample (517 profiles, one job). The two runs are not independent train/test or relevance-validation splits. Same-person versions, semantic duplicates and overlap outside the sampled documents remain unknown. Split by appropriate source entities and audit all partitions before any tuning or held-out semantic evaluation.

The local worksheet has 50 unique real pairs across ten sampled jobs, with all human relevance fields blank; it pools only hybrid top-five candidates and is not an exhaustive judgment set. `ANNOTATION_GUIDE.md` describes the human review workflow. No relevance labels have been created.
